Medicare is billing you for a year you no longer live in.

Social Security sets your Medicare premium using your tax return from two years ago — for 2026 premiums, generally your 2024 return. If you were still working then, you are being charged on income you no longer earn.

The surcharge is called IRMAA. In 2026 the standard Part B premium is $202.90 a month. Above an income threshold, that jumps to $284.10, $405.80, $527.50, $649.20, or $689.90 — plus a separate surcharge on your drug plan.

For a married couple placed in the middle bracket, that is an extra $4,869 in a single year — on money that stopped arriving when they retired.

Here is the part most people never hear. If your income dropped because of a qualifying life event — you retired, you cut your hours, you lost a pension, your spouse died, you divorced — Social Security has an official process to recalculate your premium using your current income instead.

The form is called SSA-44. It is free. It is also confusing enough that most people never file it and quietly overpay for years.

Questions people ask

Does this work if I sold a house or converted to a Roth?

Generally no, and the kit says so plainly. A sale or conversion you chose to make is not one of the eight qualifying events. That surcharge usually applies for one year and then drops off once the IRS supplies a return showing lower income. The kit explains how to make sure it actually does.

Will filing guarantee my premium goes down?

No, and nobody can promise that. Social Security makes the final determination on every request. What the kit does is make sure your request is complete, dated correctly, and supported by the right evidence — the three things that cause most requests to come back.

No. It is educational material. For your own situation, talk to a licensed tax professional or financial advisor, or a free SHIP counselor.
